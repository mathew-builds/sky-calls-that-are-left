Action log

Every action carries its reason and can be contested. A system that acts on an account, rather than advising about one, has to carry that under the UK automated decision rules from 5 February 2026.

The question this page exists to answer

Does the agent read the operational system of record, or a curated layer beside it? Both, and the line is drawn by consequence rather than by data type. That distinction is the whole design.

Shapes the conversation Which article to offer, prior contact history, the troubleshooting tree Curated layer, stated freshness budget. An hour stale changes the wording, not the outcome
Gates an action Does this person have authority, is this box on this account, is the service live Re-read from the owning system at the moment of the action, not at the start of the conversation
The customer is held to it Contract state, charges, entitlement A stale read here is a customer detriment, and in a UK regulated telco potentially a compensation event

The middle column is where staleness lives. Entitlement reconciles from billing overnight. Device state arrives from telemetry on its own cadence. A customer who upgraded yesterday exists differently in each, and the demo shows what happens when they disagree: the agent refuses to act on either and re-reads before committing. The mechanism is ordinary optimistic concurrency, a conditional write that fails if the state moved underneath it. I am describing the mechanism rather than claiming a named industry pattern, because there is not one.

Grounded in: AWS Prescriptive Guidance, Agentic AI architecture in the enterprise (applications and agents layers, the tools gateway pattern) and TM Forum's agentic AI framework on ODA, which names a Semantic Context Layer and a Human-in-the-Loop Gate that exits before any write if confirmation is missing. Accenture publish AI Refinery and the distiller agentic framework, both with public SDK docs. What I could not find was a layered reference architecture at this tier to map a contact journey onto, so the layers below borrow TM Forum's telco vocabulary rather than inventing one.
